Are the probabilities honest?

Each point is one tenth of the forecast cells, from least to most risky: what was forecast against what happened in 2025. Points on the dashed line are perfectly calibrated. The model’s points sit below it: it forecast more loss than happened, in every tenth. The trees (B4) were better calibrated, though they ranked worse.

What is weak, said plainly

  • The probabilities ran high on 2025: read the map as a ranking of risk first.
  • On the 2024 validation year the model was 4.6% below the reference; that check had only 146 tiles, but it is part of the record.
  • The 2025 test data were read twice: once for the graded backtest and once, against the protocol, for an independent re-grade of the same frozen forecasts. Nothing changed; both reads are logged below.
  • One region, one kind of event, one year ahead.

How the forecast is made

Inputs, up to 2025For each 640 m cell: five years (2021–2025) of AlphaEarth satellite embeddings, 64 numbers a year, for the cell and its neighbourhood; past forest loss; forest and land share.
→
Model M (A2)A transformer reads each cell’s five-year history together with its neighbours and gives the chance of loss the next year. Trained on 800 tiles that touch no exam tile.
→
Forecast for 2026Three independently trained copies, each calibrated on 2024, averaged. Frozen before the backtest and not refitted since.

The reference, B5, is the same network without the neighbourhood, trained the same way. It was the best of six candidates on the 2024 validation year, so the bar is the strongest simple competitor, chosen before the model’s backtest.

# Phase 0 — Look: report

Run on 2026-11-15, one day ahead of the calendar week (16–22 Nov). Every number below is
regenerated by the commands in the [README](../../README.md).

**Question:** does yearly change in the data correspond to real change on the ground, and does it
stand clear of noise?

**Answer:** yes at all three levels tested, with one correction to the plan. Change in the
embeddings lines up with change visible in Sentinel-2 imagery at both change sites and is absent at
both controls. But the rule the blueprint fixed for the threshold (99th percentile of stable sites)
gives a threshold so low that ordinary farmland and pasture exceed it almost everywhere, every year.
Gate 0 passes on its written criterion; the threshold rule must be redesigned in task 1.8 before
"changed" can be used as a training label. The decision is proposed in
[ADR 0001](../adr/0001-gate-0.md) and waits for the team's review.

## 4. Reader, orientation, quality gates (0.3, 0.4, 0.6)

- The raw files are bottom-up. The reader reads whole blocks from the raw file and flips rows. The
  result is **identical, value for value**, to a read through the supplier's north-up `.vrt`; the
  unflipped array is not. A live test repeats this on every run of `pytest -m network`.
- One block-year costs **20–40 MB** of transfer in about 66 range requests. Each request takes a
  second or more on this connection, so bands are read over eight connections at once: 10–30
  seconds per block-year instead of 90.
- The `.vrt` points at an `s3://` address and needs unsigned access switched on.

## 5. Pyramid (0.7)

The supplier's coarse levels were compared with candidates built from finer levels at the Xiong'an
block, 2021.

| Supplier level | Best candidate | Values identical | Within 1 step |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Renormalised mean of decoded L0 | 87.9% | 99.9% |
| L3 | Renormalised mean of decoded L0 | 96.7% | 100.0% |
| L6 | Renormalised mean of decoded L0 | 99.5% | 100.0% |

Nearest-neighbour sampling matches under 22% of values at any level, and plain averaging without
renormalising matches 26% at L6. **The pyramid is the mean of the decoded level-0 vectors,
renormalised to length 1.** Coarse vectors are unit length (median 1.000 at every level), so they
are valid embeddings and gate Q10 has a method to test against. This closes open question 5. One
block-year is thin evidence; repeat on several tiles in task 1.1.

## 6. Noise floor and change (0.8, 0.9)

Change `δ = 1 − e(t)·e(t+1)` for the eight consecutive year pairs. Coarse levels were built here
from level 0 by the method found in section 5.

### Noise floor at the stable sites

| Level | Desert, median | Desert, 99th | Forest interior, median | Forest interior, 99th | Pooled 99th (candidate `τ`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 (10 m) | 0.0041 | 0.0079 | 0.0080 | 0.0162 | 0.0149 |
| L3 (80 m) | 0.0037 | 0.0065 | 0.0071 | 0.0144 | 0.0133 |
| L6 (640 m) | 0.0034 | 0.0059 | 0.0066 | 0.0131 | 0.0124 |

The forest floor is twice the desert floor. Level 6 has only 256 cells per block, so its
percentiles are rough.

### Gate criterion as written

Share of cells above the pooled `τ` in the event years, against the stable share (1% by
construction; 0.0% in the desert, at most 7.8% in the forest in its worst year):

| Site | Event pairs | Share above `τ` at L0 | At L6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xiong'an | 2019–20, 2020–21, 2021–22 | 84%, 97%, 100% | 73%, 100%, 100% |
| Novo Progresso | 2019–20, 2020–21, 2023–24 | 34%, 51%, 80% | 25%, 54%, 86% |

Both exceed five times the stable share at every level. **The criterion passes.**

### Why that pass is weaker than it looks

At Xiong'an, 86% of cells are above `τ` in 2017–18, before any construction. At Novo Progresso it
is 33–39% in the quiet years. Farmland, pasture and wetland change their yearly description far
more than a desert or a closed forest does, without any change of land use. A threshold taken from
stable desert and forest therefore flags nearly all managed land, every year. As a label for
"something happened here" it is useless on exactly the land where things happen.

Two further observations:

- At Novo Progresso, 2024–25 puts 100% of cells above `τ`, forest included (median `δ` 0.054
  against 0.008–0.029 before). Either the 2024 drought and fire season changed the whole block, or
  the 2025 layer differs systematically. The 2025 files were written four months after the others.
  This needs checking on more tiles before 2025 is used as a training target.
- In the forest interior, the share above `τ` varies from 0.1% to 7.8% between year pairs. Noise is
  not constant from year to year.

### Does the change correspond to real change?

This is the part of the question the criterion does not test, so it was tested directly. Change in
imagery was measured independently (difference of normalised Sentinel-2 red, green, blue and
near-infrared between the two years' scenes, averaged to 160 m). The top tenth of that measure was
called "changed in imagery", and embedding change was scored on how well it ranks those cells.

| Site | Area under the curve, by year pair (0.5 is chance) |
|---|---|
| Xiong'an | 0.90, 0.81, 0.87, 0.78, 0.82, 0.76, 0.87, 0.89 |
| Novo Progresso | 0.88, 0.94, 0.94, 0.86, 0.85, 0.89, 0.86, 0.82 |
| Desert control | 0.52, 0.65, 0.72, 0.47, 0.69, 0.50, 0.50, 0.80 |
| Forest control | 0.53, 0.53, 0.62, 0.57, 0.49, 0.42, 0.43, 0.46 |

And the share of cells with large change, `δ > 0.2`, at level 0:

| Site | Before the event | Event years | Controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xiong'an | 2.3%, 3.2% (2017–19) | 6.7%, 9.6%, 8.0% | 0.0% in every pair but one (0.1%) |
| Novo Progresso | 0.1%, 0.2%, 0.4% (2017–20) | 1.4%, 1.4%, 1.8% (2020–23) | |

In the figures the bright areas of the change maps sit on the demolished villages, the new street
grid and the reshaped lake shore at Xiong'an, and on the new clearings and mining scars at Novo
Progresso.

Caveats: the imagery measure is crude and mixes real change with crop state and scene date. The
Sentinel-2 scenes for Xiong'an in 2017 and 2018 are from May, the rest from September–October. Two
change sites and two controls are a look, not a survey.

## 7. Difference encoding (0.11)

Nine years per site, compressed with Zstandard level 9, with and without bit shuffle.

| Site | Level | As read | Difference-encoded | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xiong'an | L0 crop | 78.7 MB | 80.3 MB | 0.98× |
| Novo Progresso | L0 crop | 80.0 MB | 72.5 MB | 1.10× |
| Desert | L0 crop | 43.1 MB | 40.5 MB | 1.06× |
| Forest interior | L0 crop | 62.5 MB | 50.9 MB | 1.23× |
| All four | L6 | — | — | 1.20–1.39× |

The round trip is exact everywhere. The blueprint hoped for 2–4×; the measured gain is
**1.0–1.4×**. Year-to-year noise in the stored integers is as hard to compress as the values
themselves. Recommendation: do not adopt difference encoding. Store values as read with Zstandard;
the saving does not pay for the extra step and the dependence of every year on the one before. The
level-6 sample (16 × 16 cells) is tiny, so re-measure on one real tile in task 1.1 before closing
this.

## 8. Changes to the plan

| # | Finding | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stable-site `τ` flags most managed land every year | Task 1.8 must define `τ` differently: per land-cover class or per cell from its own history, and probably much higher (the sites separate cleanly at `δ` of 0.1–0.2). Decide before patch selection in the streaming pass |
| 2 | 302,466 files, not 127,000 | Re-estimate transfer, time and the kept sample for the streaming pass. Blueprint section 7 updated |
| 3 | Image ids change yearly | Tile ID is zone + south-west corner. Blueprint section 8.2 needs the same change |
| 4 | Difference encoding gains 1.0–1.4× | Drop it unless task 1.1 shows otherwise; the 4–12 GB estimate for the patch store was already the as-read size divided by the hoped gain, so plan on the upper end or more |
| 5 | The 2025 layer may differ systematically | Check on more tiles before fixing folds that use 2025 as the target |
| 6 | No version tag in the files | Gate Q5 needs another source: object dates and entity tags, recorded in the manifest |
| 7 | Mirror answers each request in 1–4 s | Request count, not bytes, sets the pace. Eight connections per file works; keep the cap of 16 |
